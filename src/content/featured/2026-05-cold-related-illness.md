Using 25 years of national hospital data, the team found that the adjusted rate of hospitalizations for cold-related illness rose from 42.0 to 122.5 per 100,000 hospitalizations between 1998 and 2022.

Patients hospitalized for cold-related illness were more likely to die in the hospital, live in high-poverty areas, be uninsured or publicly insured, and have behavioral health conditions or unstable housing. The authors call for better access to behavioral health treatment, help with home heating, affordable and supportive housing, and preparation for extreme weather.
